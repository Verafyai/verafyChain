Coin-free guarantee.

There is no coin. There is no board of directors, no governance, no VC, no vesting contracts, no corrupt treasury, and no fake community funds. There is no secretive engineering team being paid by someone to build it. You can specify and get paid in any coin you want, so there is no need to release another one. There is no exchange launch and no pump and dump. Verafy Chain is just meant to bring people back to the basics of crypto: playing around, having fun, and doing something innovative. That's it.

A Verafy research concept

Contracts written in words. Validators with judgment.

Change one

Natural language contracts

A contract is a compressed intention: what should happen, under what conditions, judged against which sources. It goes to an AI jury that renders an outcome, instead of being limited to what can be written as math.

Change two

The validator becomes a harnessed AI

A validator is an AI model wrapped in a harness that handles its inputs, outputs, tools, keys, bond and smart contracts. Anyone can run one. Together they form an oracle network built from harnesses.

PrinciplesDesign

Eleven design principles

Every part of Verafy Chain and Verafy Harness is designed against these. The first five are the founding ones. When a design choice conflicts with a principle, the principle wins, or the change goes through the amendment process.

01

Payment agnostic

No dedicated token or coin, ever. Contracts pay in coins that already exist, on the chains where they already live. A harness is paid in whatever a contract offers, and its routing agent swaps and pays its own AI and hosting bills.

built payment router with a local ledger, banker with test venues
planned Solana and EVM routers

02

Natural language first

Contracts are written in words: an intention, the conditions, the sources to judge against, and the exits. AI juries rule on what code can't express. Everything that must be exact, such as payments, exits, hashes and signatures, stays exact.

built the contract format, with pinned context and declared exits

03

Embedded ethics

Ethics and behavior rules are encoded in Block 0 and enforced by the chain, not left to users. Every contract and every governance change passes the constitution check, then the ethics check. Doubt resolves toward refusal.

draft the Constitution and its Embedded Ethics
built the ethics gate and the seven amendment stages, on the devnet with scripted answers

04

Checkable and verifiable

Every ruling can be re-checked by anyone. Context is pinned by hash, votes are committed before they are revealed, every vote is signed with the model that cast it, and auditors from other labs re-run rulings. Where possible, inference is proven: re-executable open weights, hardware attestation, or a zero-knowledge proof. Neutrality is earned the same way, by juries that span labs, commit before they see each other, and are paid for dissent as well as agreement, rather than assumed.

built pins, commit and reveal, signatures, auditors and challenges
research zero-knowledge proof of inference

05

Agent friendly

Agents are the main users, so everything works without a person in the loop. Contracts, rulings and reasons are typed, machine-readable records, not just prose. Every command has JSON output. Errors and refusals say why, in words an agent can act on. Agents hold money safely through scoped keys, allowlists and spending caps, and never see a raw secret. Every action is meant to be safe to retry, with a hash on every record. Rules are meant to be discoverable: an agent should be able to read the Constitution, the ethics and the contract schema from the chain itself.

built --json output, scoped keys, caps and allowlists, retry with back-off
planned a tool interface agents can call directly, and rules readable from the chain

06

Context is evidence

Nothing a juror reads is treated as an instruction to it. Text that tries to steer, bribe or threaten a jury is grounds to stop the contract. Evidence is compiled by Prompt Guard first, so an instruction reaches a juror only as a quoted directive.

built the juror tool lock and the pre-execute gate in DeepSeek Harness, and Prompt Guard in Verafy Harness

07

No single model, no single lab

Juries span labs, so no one model's blind spots or one provider's policy decides an outcome. Any lab's model can serve, open or hosted, as long as the jury is diverse.

measured five labs ruled together and sealed a block, Nov 6, 2026

08

Nothing is erased

Rulings, refusals, challenges and amendments are appended, never edited. Block 0 is never rewritten. Anyone can answer "what did the chain decide, and why" for any point in time.

built append-only challenges: affirm, overturn, supersede

09

Legible to people

Agents do the work, but people can read all of it. Every rule, contract, ruling and reason is in plain words next to its hash, so a person can audit the chain without running a node.

built the words live next to the block, fetchable by hash

10

Open, forkable, no owner

MIT licensed, built in public, with no foundation, no VC, no treasury and no permission needed. Anyone can run a harness, run a node, or copy the whole thing.

built open repositories, MIT licensed

11

Honest about what runs

Every number is labeled measured, modeled, vendor-reported or illustrative. A concept is called a concept, and a prototype a prototype. Verafy Chain is not a running network.

built labels on every page

§ 0Constitution

Block 0 is a constitution, with its ethics embedded

The first block of Verafy Chain holds its constitution and its Embedded Ethics, written in words and built into the chain itself. It is meant to be ratified by the first jury consensus: harnesses running models from several labs rule on it the same way they rule on any contract, and it seals only if they clear the bar.

Block 0Genesis. Constitution and Embedded Ethics.Draft. Not sealed.
  1. No foundation.
  2. No venture capital.
  3. No coin. Verafy Chain never mints, launches or sells a token. Contracts pay in coins that already exist.
  4. No fundraising.
  5. No secret engineering team. The work happens in public, in open repositories.
  6. No community fund to be captured or corrupted.
  7. No treasury.
  8. All governance follows this written constitution and is judged by a panel of agents.
  9. Every governance change passes the constitution check, then the ethics check, before it is judged on its merits.
  10. Disputes and amendments go to panels of agents and are judged against this constitution. An amendment passes seven stages, from proposal and public comment through both checks, a merit ruling and a challenge window, before it seals. It is appended as a new ruling. Block 0 itself is never edited, and its core clauses can only be made stricter.

This is a draft. Once a jury seals it, it governs the chain from that block forward, and every governance question, dispute and amendment follows from it.

Embedded Ethics

An ethics gate screens every contract and every governance proposal before it can enter a block. A panel of agents from several labs allows, refuses or escalates, and doubt resolves toward refusal. Harm to children, violence markets, weapons of mass harm, targeting private people, fraud and laundering are never allowed. built on the devnet with scripted answers; it governs nothing until the Constitution is ratified.

§ 1Change one

Contracts as compressed intentions

A smart contract today can only act on what someone could express as math. Most of what people want enforced isn't math: a policy, a promise, a claim, a condition about the world. Verafy Chain lets the contract be the intention itself, written in plain language and pinned by hash. Each request is distributed to a jury of harnessed validators, and the outcome they agree on is what the contract does.

Questions

Something with an answer that can be found.

Did the bill pass the Senate by Nov 30?

Intentions

What a person or group wants to happen, and when.

Pay out if my flight lands 3+ hours late.

Ideas

A claim or proposal to be weighed, not just looked up.

Does this milestone meet Article 4?

Forecasts

A view about the future, given as a probability.

Chance of a rate cut in March?

The intention is compressed: a short instruction plus pinned context, not a conversation. That keeps it cheap to distribute, easy to hash, and the same for every juror.

What goes into an Actually Smart Contract

Every Actually Smart Contract has the same four parts. Context says what to judge against. The question says what to decide. Payment funds the jury that decides it. Exits are the only things the contract can do once it has an answer.

16:9, fixed frame. Loops.

Context

Links, text, policies, rules, documents. Each is pinned by hash so every juror reads the same thing.

Question

The intention, compressed to what must be decided, plus the input for this request.

Payment

Escrow that pays the jury, with the jury's size, the labs it must span, and the bar it must clear.

Exits

The outcomes the jury may choose and what each does: pay, refund, hold, or call a contract on another chain.

§ 2Change two

A validator is an AI model in a harness

On today's chains a validator is a machine that re-runs code and votes on blocks. On Verafy Chain a validator is an AI model wrapped in a harness. The model brings judgment. The harness does everything else a validator does: it takes requests in, loads and checks their context, gives the model safe tools, signs and posts votes, holds the bond, and runs the smart contracts that act on rulings. § 3Contracts in the harness

Smart contracts live inside the harness

Each harness carries a contract runtime. When a ruling seals, the harness runs whatever the contract declared for that outcome: release escrow, pay a market's winning side, record a forecast score, or call a program on another chain. Ordinary smart contracts don't go away. They become the exact, mechanical part of a contract whose judgment is done in words.

Declared outcomes

A contract lists its possible outcomes and what each one does. The jury can only pick from that list.

YES: pay yes-holders. NO: pay no-holders. UNCLEAR: refund all.

Triggers

An outcome can fire a call on another chain, signed by the quorum of harnesses that ruled.

On "pay claim": call delay_cover.settle() on Solana

Checked by all

Every harness runs the same runtime, so every harness can check that a payout matches the outcome exactly.

diff must conserve totals and match the outcome
§ 4Run your own

Anyone can run a harness

Verafy Chain is an oracle network, but its nodes are harnesses rather than data feeds. You choose a model, run the harness on your own hardware or with your own API keys, post a bond, and start taking seats on juries. The network doesn't care which lab your model comes from. It cares that juries span several labs, and that your track record holds up under audit.

A model

Open weights on your own GPU, or a hosted model through an API. You commit to one version.

The harness

One package: intake, loader, tools, guardrails, signer, contract runtime, outbox.

A bond and a wallet

Stake that sizes how much value your votes can decide, and a payment wallet the routing agent uses to pay for the AI and the hosting.

A specialty

Optional. Harnesses can advertise domains, such as sports results or earnings calls, and earn seats there.

The validator package is Verafy Harness. Its site covers the blocks, the routing agent that pays for the AI, what runs today, and how to run one.

§ 6Close enough

Rulings don't have to be exact. They have to clear the bar.

Judgment is fuzzy, so Verafy Chain doesn't pretend otherwise. Every contract sets how much the jury must agree. A ruling at 85% agreement on a contract that requires 70% is final. A ruling at 60% isn't: it goes to a larger jury in the next block, or the contract falls back to its safe default.

How agreement is measured

  • Verdicts: the share of the jury, weighted by stated confidence, that picked the winning outcome.
  • Forecasts: how tightly the probabilities cluster around the median.
  • Graded answers: how many landed on the same grade or the one next to it.

Checked after the fact

  • Auditor harnesses re-run a random sample of sealed rulings with fresh labs.
  • Anyone can challenge a ruling with new evidence during its window, or later as precedent review.
  • Forecasts are scored against what happened, building each harness's track record.
§ 7Proof of Judgment

A block exists only when a jury rules

Nothing mines or stakes its way into producing a block. A new block is added only when harnesses have ruled on the requests waiting for it, and the block is made of those rulings. The money that creates the block pays for the judgment inside it, and each block commits to its rulings and to the block before it.

Like proof of work

  • A block costs real resources to produce: model compute.
  • Anyone can join the pool by running a harness.
  • Each block is chained to the last by hash.

Unlike proof of work

  • The work is useful. Every unit pays for a decision someone asked for.
  • A hash is cheap to check. A judgment is not, so security leans on bonds, audits and challenges.
  • Small proofs of inference would close that gap.

When a proven block closes, and who can read the queue

An L1 contract seals as soon as a jury rules. An L2 contract, whose votes carry proofs of inference, waits in a queue, because one proof is cheap (about a cent of GPU time, modeled) and what makes a small block uneconomic is fixed cost: warm-up and idle GPU hours. The block closes when the queue is big enough that warm-up is at most 10% of its modeled cost and the escrow covers that cost plus margin, or when a contract has waited its maximum (3,600 s by default). Both defaults are illustrative and proposed; they change only by amendment. The cost is split pro rata by proven requests, and the author sees a quote before funding. Prototype on the devnet: built and tested.

While a contract waits, it is sealed 2 of 3 (devnet) to the node set: only its escrow reference and payment metadata are readable. Nodes release their decryption shares only after the jury is drawn from the parent block hash, and the plaintext and its pins go to the availability layer at reveal. Two limits: the proving machine sees the plaintext it proves, so encryption protects the queue, not the prover; and the escrow stays on its source chain, where the amount is public unless that chain supports confidential amounts. Real distributed key generation is planned, not built. How batching and the encrypted queue work

Proving an L2 ruling never holds up the commit. Jurors commit and reveal as usual, then each attaches a proof hash and uploads its proof before a deadline. A vote whose proof is missing or fails is dropped, agreement is measured again against the bar, and a fault is recorded against that harness's bond; a ruling that falls below the bar escalates. Only proof hashes go on chain. Prototype on the devnet with a mock prover, which exercises the flow and is not a proof of inference: built and tested. A real prover (DeepProve or FlashProve) is planned.

§ 8Who pays, who earns

People pay for answers. Harness operators earn by giving good ones.

The network only exists if someone needs a decision badly enough to fund it. Prediction markets need resolutions, fact checkers need volume, forecasting apps need a crowd that runs around the clock, and contracts on other chains need decisions they can't make in code. Each funds requests in escrow, and that escrow pays the harnesses.

§ 8aNo coin

There is no token. There is no need for one.

Verafy Chain is coin agnostic on purpose. It runs on whatever asset people want to put into it. A contract can be paid in USDC on Solana, ETH on Ethereum, or any asset on any chain with contracts, and any wallet works. The point is to build something useful regardless of the crypto.

Money stays where it is

The requester funds escrow on their own chain. Verafy Chain records the ruling, and a quorum-signed trigger releases the escrow there, in the same asset.

A payment router in every harness

Each harness plugs in a payment router and sets what it accepts, for example USDC on Solana. It takes seats only on contracts it will be paid for.

"payments": { "router": "solana-usdc", "accept": [{ "chain": "solana", "asset": "USDC" }] }

Paid in what was paid in

Jurors, auditors, storage and sealers are paid their share in the contract's own asset. Bonds are posted the same way.

What the money pays for

Inference

Each juror runs its AI model on the contract. Tokens at a provider, or GPU time on its own hardware.

Running the harness

The machine, the network, uptime, a pinned model, and the capital locked in the bond.

Audits and storage

Re-running sampled rulings with another lab's model, and keeping every prompt and piece of evidence fetchable by hash.

Bookkeeping

Sealing blocks, checking every signature and diff, and the source chain's fee to release the escrow.

The routing agent pays the bills

Every harness carries a routing agent built from four blocks. The keyblock holds every API key and wallet key encrypted and issues short-lived scoped keys, so nothing else in the harness touches a raw secret. The banker accepts any digital token, swaps one for another at the best quote on approved venues, and funds payments in whatever token a supplier takes. The API router plugs into AI APIs, external API routers, proving services and cloud hosts, and pays each one. The payment wallet is where earnings land and bills are paid from, within the operator's caps and never touching the bond.

In the prototype the router is a local ledger that records every payout without moving funds. The Solana and EVM routers are on the roadmap. The whole policy, including what we will never do, is in Coin Agnostic.

§ 9Where the words live

The block holds fingerprints. The words live next to it.

Instructions and evidence can run to many pages, and a block has to stay small. So a block stores the short request inline and everything else as hashes. The full text sits in an availability layer kept by storage harnesses, and each juror's context loader fetches it and checks every hash before the model reads a word.

§ 13Open problems

Why this is a concept and not a network

Proof of which model ran

Proofs exist for one open model today. Until more can be proven, trust rests on bonds and audits.

Evidence you can't trust

A jury is only as good as what it reads. Forged sources remain the weakest point.

Liveness

If several providers go down at once, juries can't reach quorum. The chain needs a fallback that holds rather than guesses.

Model drift

A model update can read the same words differently. Harnesses pin versions, and the network needs a rule for retiring them.

Who makes the models

Anyone can run a harness, but a few labs make the strongest models. That is a new kind of centralization to design around.

Setting the bar

Too low and wrong rulings pass; too high and nothing settles. Each market will need its own number.
